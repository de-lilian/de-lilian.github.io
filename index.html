About

Making sense of the loop between a brain and its surrounding world

I'm a doctoral researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics in Tübingen, in the Systems Neuroscience & Neuroengineering group of Drew Robson and Jennifer Li. My PhD asks how animals decide when and where to forage — and how the brain weighs its own internal state against changing resources in the outside world.

I work with larval zebrafish because they let me record whole-brain activity while an animal freely behaves. Day to day, that means a lot of code: building analysis pipelines in Julia and Python, wrangling large-scale neural and behavioral data, and turning it into decoding models, dimensionality reductions, and visualizations that actually tell a story.

Before the PhD I trained in cognitive science and neural & behavioural sciences, with a study abroad in Brazil and detours through eye-tracking, EEG, and microscopy. I like being a generalist: bridging experiments, engineering, and analysis — and explaining complex ideas so different people can act on them.
