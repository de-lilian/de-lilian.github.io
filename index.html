About

Finding signal in large, noisy, high-dimensional data

I'm a doctoral researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics in Tübingen, in the Systems Neuroscience & Neuroengineering group of Drew Robson and Jennifer Li. My PhD builds statistical and machine-learning models of behaviour and brain-wide activity — decoding hidden states from large, noisy recordings.

Day to day, that means a lot of code: reproducible pipelines in Python and Julia, wrangling large-scale time-series, and turning them into decoding models, dimensionality reductions, and honest, cross-validated results. I care about statistical rigour — permutation testing, multiple-comparison correction, and avoiding data leakage.

I'm drawn to quantitative finance because it's the same core problem: extracting signal from large, noisy, fast-changing data. Outside research I've been a long-term ETF investor for years, and I'm keen to bring a scientific, evidence-based approach to financial markets.

🧠 PhD · Ongoing

Decoding brain states from large, noisy recordings

Python / JuliaDecodingCross-validation

Modular pipelines that decode behavioural and environmental states from whole-brain recordings (thousands of units × time): PCA, SVM and logistic-regression classifiers, permutation testing with FDR correction, and strict train/test separation to avoid leakage.

Circadian organization of sleep substates

GMMPyTorchCross-validation

Co-authored Nature Communications study: classifying sleep substates with Gaussian mixture models, modelling state probabilities with a PyTorch neural-network logistic regression (cross-validated, with input-ablation importance), and decoding time with a regularized linear decoder.

Nature Communications ↗
👁️ 2024

Individual variability & stability in spatial memory

StatisticsEye-trackingBehaviour

First-author study: statistical analysis of eye-tracking data showing that individuals differ in memory-rehearsal strategy yet stay strikingly stable over time (test–retest). Published in Journal of Vision.
